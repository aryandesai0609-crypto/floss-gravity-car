Where the energy goes

Gear ratio sweep: distance vs axle-to-wheel ratio

Uses the "CD Long-Hauler" chassis. Below a critical ratio the floss can't pull hard enough to beat friction and the car never moves (the cliff). Above it, distance is nearly flat, then slowly drops because higher speeds waste more energy (air drag, weight impact, wheel spin). Best choice: the lowest ratio that still starts reliably, with about 2x margin.

The four designs

Physics model. Car position x; floss unwound s (ds = v·r/R dt); weight height H = |L − s|/n, where L = n·h is the floss length and n is the floss pulley ratio. Equation of motion (Lagrange): (Meff + mwk²)·a + mw·k·k′·v² = ±mwg·k − Floss, where k = r/(nR) and Meff = M + ΣI/R². Floss = Crr·Mg + μb·Mg·rb/R + ½ρCdAv² + (1−η)·mwg·k (pulley/guide friction). With a release catch the floss drops off at the bottom. Without one, the axle rewinds the floss and lifts the weight back up (the yo-yo brake). Coefficients come from Engineering ToolBox (rolling and sliding friction), Valentini et al. 2025 (floss strength), OpenStax (wheel and axle, pulleys), and HyperPhysics (rotational inertia). Real cars usually go 20 to 40% shorter than ideal models because of misalignment and wobble.
